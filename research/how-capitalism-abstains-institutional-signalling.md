# How Capitalism Abstains — institutional varieties and diplomatic signalling

**Author:** Athena Biju  
**Research paper:** *How Capitalism Abstains: How do institutional varieties of capitalism shape the volatility and abstention-adjusted misalignment of UN voting relationships among major economies?*  
**Context:** Comparing Economic Systems, Fall 2025, Constructor University; submitted 4 December 2025; instructor Prof. Dr. Tobias ten Brink.  
**Status:** Completed course research paper. Results and interpretation have not been independently replicated or peer-reviewed.

## Research question

Can different institutional forms of capitalism be associated with distinctive ways of managing alignment, abstention and uncertainty in United Nations General Assembly voting?

The paper connects comparative political economy, varieties of capitalism and international political economy. It explores liberal market economies (LMEs), coordinated market economies (CMEs), and state-permeated economies (SMEs) as a conceptual framework for interpreting voting patterns.

## Data and empirical design described in the paper

- **Primary empirical source:** historical UNGA roll-call voting records (the paper cites Voeten et al., 2017).
- **Window:** 1946–2022.
- **Comparative actors:** India, China, Russia/USSR, the United States, and a constructed France–Germany (EU) voting proxy.
- **Measurement:** dyadic voting misalignment, year-to-year squared changes in misalignment, and an abstention-adjustment contrast called *volatility–delta*.
- **Interpretation:** negative and positive volatility–delta values are investigated as possible indicators of whether abstentions smooth or amplify changes in alignment.

The author's paper reports distinctive descriptive patterns across its selected actors. These are **interpretations proposed within a course-paper framework**, not independently established causal estimates.

## How the V-Dem notebook relates — and how it does not

An **unpublished exploratory V-Dem notebook** separately examines the V-Dem country-year dataset, including indicators such as accountability and corruption. It builds candidate governance/opacity measures and explores historical trajectories for selected states.

This is a potentially useful *extension* of the UNGA paper: domestic institutional measures could be compared with diplomatic misalignment or abstention dynamics. **The V-Dem indicators were not the primary data used to estimate the reported UNGA voting results.** The notebook does not independently validate the paper's volatility–delta claims, and the two datasets have not been demonstrated to be merged into a tested empirical model.

### Necessary methodological work before publishing the V-Dem analysis as evidence

1. **Define the construct and confirm directionality.** Accountability and several corruption indicators are not interchangeable direct measures of institutional opacity; consult V-Dem variable definitions and uncertainty intervals.
2. **Avoid false cross-country comparability.** Some experimental transforms min–max-normalise within each country over 2013–2023; such within-country scores reflect relative change and are not directly comparable *levels* of opacity across countries.
3. **Specify a coherent sample and time window.** The UNGA paper covers 1946–2022, while one V-Dem extraction is restricted to 2013–2023; historical plots in other notebook cells cover longer periods.
4. **Resolve composite weighting, missingness and historical state definitions.** A simple unweighted average of differently oriented and correlated indicators should not be treated as a validated institutional index.
5. **Test rather than presume the link.** Any association between institutional covariates and diplomatic signalling requires an explicit join, specification, robustness checks and careful treatment of confounding.
6. **Reproduce from an available dataset.** The notebook refers to an external V-Dem CSV; that file and a pinned environment are not distributed here.

The V-Dem notebook is therefore **not published as a completed research result** in this portfolio.

## Interpretation and limitations of the UNGA paper

The paper explicitly acknowledges that its inference is constrained by vote coding, dyadic construction, institutional typologies and the absence of causal identification. Additional issues warrant sensitivity checks: treatment of abstention versus absence; the coding of France and Germany as an 'EU' proxy; changing country memberships and USSR/Russia continuity; the assignment of particular countries to varieties-of-capitalism types; and whether volatility differences persist when adjusting for issue area or shifts in the UN agenda.

The paper's conceptual insight is a research hypothesis: **abstention can convey diplomatic flexibility and uncertainty rather than being treated only as missing agreement**. The descriptive measures are useful starting points; the project does not establish that domestic capitalism *causes* voting patterns, nor does it demonstrate a tradable investment signal.

## Relevance to markets research

For an investor, the adjacent questions concern geopolitical alignment, policy discretion, sanctions exposure and institutional uncertainty. Translating them into investment decisions would require clearly specified economic transmission channels, appropriate financial data and out-of-sample validation; the academic paper itself does not establish investment performance.
